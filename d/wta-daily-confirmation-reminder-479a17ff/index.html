What changed

Before is the production flow as it runs tonight. After is the new flow in the sandbox, saved in its production shape.

The flows being replaced before

Before (days 5 to 10, v9, production). One Update step per line item. Salesforce groups the line items 200 at a time and runs the groups side by side, which is what collided on September 11.

object: line item
batch size: empty

Before (v9 start element). Object is the line item. Four conditions carry the day-10 rule. Max Batch Size, outlined in red, is empty, so the platform default of 200 applies.

Before (days 1 to 4, v1, production). The second flow, identical in shape, runs five minutes later. Both are retired by the change.

The new flow after

fault path: retry once, then log

After (Daily Confirmation Reminder, sandbox v4). Start on the event, one Update step, and a fault path outlined in red: Retry Check, Bump Retry back into the update, then Log Fault. The version shown is the production shape and is saved inactive in the sandbox.

object: event
batch size: 20

After (start element). Object is now the Opportunity, outlined in red. The entry window is the event date from 400 days past to 400 days ahead, wider than the reminder window on purpose (see the callout). The capture shows the September 11 values (10 past, 30 ahead); the numbers changed on September 12 after Jeff's note. Max Batch Size, outlined in red, is 20 events per group.

After (Update Line Items). Condition 1 ties the line items to the triggering event. Conditions 2 to 5 reproduce the existing rule: days 1 to 9 and not Accepted, or day 10. The two flags set are unchanged.

Why the event window is wider than 1 to 10 days

A line item's own date can differ from its event's date, sometimes by weeks (Jeff's September 12 note). Across the last 365 days of production data, 1,920 line items were dated after their event ended, 341 of them by more than 10 days, and the extremes were 335 days after and 332 days before. A window of 400 days past to 400 days ahead finds every one of them. It reads about 7,000 events a night instead of 700, but each event only checks its own line items and writes nothing unless a line item is 1 to 10 days out, so the extra events cost reads, not writes. The 10-past, 30-ahead window from September 11 would have missed 406 line items a year.

Retry is immediate

The plan called for a 30 second pause before the retry. The platform rejected a timed pause in this flow type during validation, so the retry runs at once. A second failure logs one row and ends the interview. No change to what gets updated.

Sandbox copy versus production copy

The sandbox test versions carry one extra start condition that limits them to the fixture account, and a smaller batch size for the first run. The production file has neither. Everything else is identical.

Why batch size alone was not enough context

Earlier on September 11 the current line-item flow was tested with batch size 25 on 30 line items of one event. Run 1 passed. Run 2 reversed the group order and failed the same way production did: the larger group held the event record 14.6 seconds, the smaller group waited 10.2 seconds and was cancelled. That is the finding that moved the design to one interview per event.
