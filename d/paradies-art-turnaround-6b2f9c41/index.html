241 requests created since 1 July 2026 and closed by 13 October. Difference is the median actual minus the median asked for. All day figures are medians, so half of each group came in faster than the number shown and half slower.

What this shows

  • About seven in ten requests finish after the date first asked for. The typical request is asked for in 14 days and takes 22.
  • September work is meeting its dates far more often. Of the requests created in July, 75% missed the date first asked for. In August it was 87%. In September it is between 41% and 54%, a range because 22 of them are still in work. August and September asked for the same 9 days, so the dates did not get longer to do it.
  • Complexity matters more than decoration type. A hard screen print takes 49 days, a medium one 17. Every one of the 15 hard embroidery requests missed its date, typically by 33 days.
  • Medium embroidery gets the shortest dates of anything. Four days requested, seventeen actual, so 88% of them miss.
  • Work sent back for revision adds about 17 days.
  • Claude already judges complexity when a request arrives, so this is known at the moment a due date gets set.

What we could do with it

  • Claude suggests a due date on the request, the way it suggests an artist today, from the complexity it already rates and the decoration type on the request. Anyone can override it.
  • Show the typical time for that kind of work, and let whoever sets the date judge for themselves.
  • Flag a request when the date asked for sits well under the typical time.
  • Record art style too, so within a couple of months the estimates can account for that as well.
  • Leave it as is and look again once more work has closed.

How the numbers were checked

  • The close date is the last time a request moved to Closed in its history. The Closed Date field only started filling in on 27 July, and it keeps the first close, so a request reopened and closed again would look faster than it was.
  • Left out of the table: 171 closed requests with no complexity recorded, mostly from July before Claude rated every request. They are still counted in the month figures above.
  • Also left out: 59 requests entered already closed, which cannot be timed, and 8 whose original due date is before the day they were created.
  • Some rows rest on six or seven requests, so read those as early direction rather than settled numbers.
  • Every figure was worked out twice, by two separate scripts from the same export, and they agree.
