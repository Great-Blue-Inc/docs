348 requests created since 1 July 2026 and closed by 2 November. Difference is the median actual minus the median asked for. All day figures are medians, so half of each group came in faster than the number shown and half slower. Rows with fewer than 10 requests are early direction only.

What this shows

  • About two in three requests finish after the date first asked for (231 of 348). The typical request is asked for in 14 days and takes 21. When a request does miss, it misses by a median of 19 days. On 14 October it was 72% (162 of 226); the 122 requests closed since then missed 57% of the time (69 of 122).
  • Complexity still matters far more than decoration type. Easy requests take 13 days, medium 19 and hard 50.5. By decoration type alone the middle half of the groups sit between 17 and 22 days. Hard requests missed their date 59 times out of 66 (89%); in the 17 hard requests closed since 14 October, 16 missed, by a median of 62 days.
  • Month by month, of requests created in July 77% missed the date first asked for (186 of 245 timed). August was 88% (83 of 95). September was 49% (60 of 123). October is too early to call: of 169 created, 56 have closed (15 missed), 56 are still in work (18 already past their date), so the final figure sits somewhere between 29% and 63%. The faster requests close first, so October will read better than it should for a while.
  • The date asked for matters. Of requests asked for within a week, 81% missed (81 of 100). Of those given two weeks, 55% (50 of 91).
  • Medium embroidery still gets the shortest dates of anything. Four days requested, seventeen actual, so 89% of them miss (33 of 37).
  • Work sent back for revision adds about 11 days (143 of the 348 were sent back at least once).
  • Claude already judges complexity when a request arrives, so this is known at the moment a due date gets set.

A test of the idea

Set a suggested date for each complexity from the requests closed by 13 October: the number of days that about 7 in 10 of them finished within (easy 21, medium 30, hard 75). Applied to the 122 requests that closed after that, it would have been met 85 times (70%). The dates actually asked for were met 53 times (43%). Adding decoration type to the rule did not improve it (84 of 122), so complexity is the part that matters.

What we could do with it

  • Claude suggests a due date on the request, the way it suggests an artist today, from the complexity it already rates (about 3 weeks easy, 4 weeks medium, 11 weeks hard, based on what 7 in 10 of each have met). Decoration type did not add anything. Anyone can override it.
  • Show the typical time for that kind of work, and let whoever sets the date judge for themselves.
  • Flag a request when the date asked for sits well under the typical time.
  • Record art style too, which is not recorded today, so it cannot be tested yet. Within a couple of months we can see whether it sharpens the estimates.
  • Leave it as is. Dates would keep being missed about two times in three.

How the numbers were checked

  • The close date is the last time a request moved to Closed in its history. The Closed Date field only started filling in on 27 July, and it keeps the first close, so a request reopened and closed again would look faster than it was.
  • Left out of the table: 171 closed requests with no complexity recorded, mostly from July before Claude rated every request. They are still counted in the month figures above.
  • Also left out: 59 requests entered already closed, which cannot be timed, and 8 whose original due date is before the day they were created.
  • Some rows rest on five to nine requests, so read those as early direction rather than settled numbers.
  • Every figure was worked out twice, by two separate scripts from the same export, and they agree.
