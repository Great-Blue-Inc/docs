1. Fresh public books

These are public bid books that we never used to tune the engine. They are the fairest test of how Blue Bids does on a new job. Score = share of quote lines where the draft picked a product that meets the spec.

Public set 1 (3 books, 67 lines)Public set 3 (3 books, 38 lines per run)75% target

The Nov 10 result combines two full runs (61 of 76 lines). The bar on that point is the 90% confidence range, 71.8% to 86.7%. Single runs vary by a few points because the model does not answer the same way every time; that is why the two-run figure is the one we report. For scale: picking a random catalog product of the right type scores 8.7%.

5. What we learned about measuring

Early testing showed that parts of our first score measured the wrong thing. We corrected the score twice and made it stricter once. Each change is listed here, with the reason.

DateChangeWhy
Nov 4From “the winner's exact product” to “a product that meets the spec”Specs usually allow several products (“or equal”). The winning estimator's choice among them is often a brand preference, not the only correct answer. Scoring a valid, spec-compliant pick as wrong measured how well we guessed one person's preference, not whether the quote was right. The spec items are scored against keys written blind, before the engine ran.
Nov 5Added devices that appear only on the drawingsA real quote must include them. This made the test harder, not easier: it is why book M1 shows a lower “including drawings” score in section 2.
Nov 5Left out products named after one specific jobOn book M1, one large bundle in the winning quote was named for that job alone. No spec page or drawing names it, so only someone who had already seen the winning quote could pick it. Counting it measured access to the answer, not quoting skill.
Before the updateAfter the update75% target

The book M1 rows are one run, scored both ways. The book M2 rows are its best run under the first score and its two-run average under the updated score. The public-book scores in section 1 did not change rules at any point.

What changed, in order

  • Nov 3: exact model numbers in the spec are matched first.
  • Nov 4: past winning quotes suggest products; round 3 engine frozen for fair testing.
  • Nov 5: vector symbol counter on the drawings; spec reading moved to batch processing (27% cheaper per run).
  • Nov 9: wider product search; named-model fix; one estimator question for the camera system.
  • Nov 10: long answers split instead of cut off; two full runs confirm 80.3% on fresh books.

What is next

  • Run new real job folders twice each, to confirm the fresh-book result on more books.
  • Exact quantities: the right product is now usually picked; getting the exact count on every line is the next area of work.
  • Re-check run-to-run spread on the new books; add a second opinion only on high-risk picks if the spread stays.
