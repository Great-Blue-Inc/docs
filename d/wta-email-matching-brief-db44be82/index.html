The demo and the build

In progress, dev org

The hard part is already working. What remains for production is hardening, tuning, and real ingestion.

What the in-progress demo already shows low level

  • Channel-agnostic matching engine: sender (email address or phone number) → Contact/Lead → their open Opportunities → scored by event-date signals and word-boundary name/venue tokens → confidence plus ranked candidates.
  • Proof (a): single-opp client email auto-matched, confidence 1.00.
  • Proof (b): planner email across two clients lands in the review queue with ranked candidates.
  • Proof (c): SMS from +12025550143 matched a contact whose phone is stored as (202) 555-0143. Phone normalization proven with mismatched formats.
  • Proof (d): "Park Plaza" scored 55 vs "Parkinson" 25. A naive substring match would have false-matched. Ours did not.
  • Review dashboard LWC shows both channels with one-click "Confirm this Opp." Synthetic WTA-shaped data only. No WTA org was touched.

05

Architecture: Twilio and AWS

Twilio findings verified

Email stays native in Salesforce. Twilio powers SMS. AWS only enters if we productize.

Twilio, for SMS

  • ~$0.011/message all-in, roughly $12/mo at WTA volume, number rental ~$1.15/mo.
  • Key verified finding: inbound texts work the day the number is provisioned. A2P 10DLC registration gates outbound only (Twilio changelog, quoted in research).
  • Outbound production sending waits on brand + campaign approval: officially 1–5 business days, realistically 1–3 weeks at current queues. One-time fees ~$4 + $15.
  • Never promise live outbound SMS in week one. Inbound capture can go early.

AWS, only if we productize

  • Not needed for the MVP. At 12 users, native Apex handles matching trivially.
  • If we take the co-own/resell path, an AWS-backed matching service becomes the right production architecture: multi-tenant scale, ML headroom, one backbone for email and text.
  • Email capture stays native in Salesforce either way. Permanence in their org is our differentiator against MME's 45-day external window.

Commercial structure

Numbers needed from Josh

Both options are drafted in the client proposal with $X placeholders. Offering both lets WTA self-select on ownership versus simplicity.

Option A: build fee + co-owned IP + retainer recommended lead

One-time build fee, WTA and Great Blue jointly own the IP, small monthly service retainer. This directly answers Jeff's "reliant on Great Blue for forty years" objection (the code lives in their org and they own it) while keeping our right to resell the matching engine to other clients. This was Josh's instinct and the call transcript supports it.

Option B: subscription alternative

Low monthly fee undercutting Match My Email (roughly $10/user territory, or a flat per-org rate). We own, host, and maintain. No upfront cost for WTA. Wins if Robert balks at a build fee.
