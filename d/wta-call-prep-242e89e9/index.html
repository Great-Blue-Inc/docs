The mechanism, if he wants to go deeper low level

The flow has two paths that write the same field. The correct one already exists and has always worked. The formula is the outlier sitting beside it.

The formula is a priority ladder. Step two is the defect.

IF(NOT(ISBLANK(Referral_Contact_Name__c)),   formReferralContactandReferralAccount,
IF(NOT(ISBLANK(Act_They_Saw_Heard__c)),     Act_They_Saw_Heard__c,      <-- writes the Id

The correct name is written only when the act is first set. Rule logic on Talent_they_Saw_Heard is (1 AND 4 AND 5 AND 6) OR (2 AND 3), where 2 and 3 mean prior value null and current value populated. Every later save falls to the formula, which is why a record starts correct and flips to an Id.

02

Invoice reminder alert links nowhere

Fix proven in sandbox Not yet reported to him

Shana forwarded the alert 8/26. Diagnosed and proven the same day. Nothing has gone back to him.

Lead with this

He does not know this one is solved. Three of his clients are also getting no payment reminders at all right now, which is the more urgent half and is not fixed by the flow change.

What happens high level

Every reminder in the daily flow sends to the invoice Billing Contact. Three invoices have no Billing Contact, so the send fails with "Recipient ID is required when using templates". That failure fires the alert Shana received. The alert then builds its record link out of the same missing Billing Contact, so it has nothing to point at and arrives as a bare Salesforce address. It fails and links nowhere on exactly the occasions it fires.

Not a recent change. The formula is byte-identical back to version 21, created September 2025, and appears unchanged in two versions Jeff himself last modified.

The change low level

One formula resource, ContactRecordLink, feeding all eight fault alert bodies. A single edit, not eight. The eight send actions are untouched, so who receives customer reminders does not change.

Calendar reliability

Closed and holding

Crash fixed 8/16. Clean since. Raise only if he does.

If it comes up high level

The Saturday invite job hit a platform limit on the first ever batch over 25 rows. The fix raised the existing recursion guard around the write-backs in all three queueables, so the trigger cannot re-enter from inside a job. Deployed to prod 8/16 with 145 of 145 tests passing and rollback source captured first.

Proven three ways: red and green in sandbox on parity code, the exact failing load replayed clean, and live traffic since. Jeff confirmed the event links from the CSV.

The "third time" framing, if he raises it low level

Three different failure modes in one pipeline, not one problem recurring. April was a duplicate-events race, fixed by dedup. July was write-back loss under record lock, fixed by retry and the 25-row cap. August was chaining meeting an armed email flag on the first oversized batch. April's dedup work is what saved August, since the 25 orphaned events were reused and no duplicate was ever visible to a user.
