Slide 1

Bid Quoting

Contractors that install cabling, access control, cameras and alarms bid projects from the spec book. Today an estimator reads hundreds of pages and builds the quote by hand. Bid Quoting produces the quote draft for review, and every number traces to a spec sentence, a won job, or a drawing tag.

Slide 2

From posting to requirements

A script scans the Texas DOT bid postings and flags specs with a cabling or security section. After the scrape, those pages are uploaded and a draft is produced. Drawings are attached in the app. Claude reads the PDF in 20-page pieces and returns each requirement with its section and source sentence. Daingerfield: 30 pages, 275 requirements, 4 minutes, 60 cents.

Slide 3

Matching, price and quantity

Each requirement is searched against part number, description and manufacturer, and won-job products rank higher. Claude picks based on product type, performance category, approved manufacturer, then won history. If nothing passes, nothing is drafted and the requirement goes to Needs your call with its closest candidates. Across four graded books the draft plus its alternatives covers about 70 percent of won material dollars; the auto-drafted lines alone about 60. Price is the last won job, then list price. Quantity comes from the spec when it states a count, otherwise from the last won job, marked for confirmation. Labor is an estimate. We graded it today against CamNet's history: not reliable yet.

Slide 4

Review and drawings

Every line has one status that says what a person must do. A line opens to its sentence, its price source and its alternatives. The drawing counter reads the text layer of the plans; a vision model misread arrows and callouts as devices. Use count puts a count onto a line. Nothing commits on its own.

Slide 5

Create the quote

Pick the account and press Create quote. The lines write to CPQ or the standard Quote, and the draft locks. The quote is the record from then on.

Slide 6

Web build

The same React screen on a Python API server with SQLite, for a client without Salesforce. They bring three CSVs: catalog with prices, accounts, won history. Spec and drawings upload the same way, and quotes export to PDF or CSV.
